SPACE ELEVATORS

A space elevator is a concept that is am a long tube or connection that goes from the earth – located around the equator – until it can reach a geostationary orbit - around 35,786km. This is when the structure orbits with a period equal to one day and has a rotation rate equal to the rotation rate of the earth’s surface meaning to a terrestrial observer the object appears stationary.

Its structure will consist of a base station in which people can travel on earth to it. This space station also could be on water which might even have provide use in terms of high-altitude support and control. At the top, however, will have a station which will be the counterweight or centre of mass for the elevator, and thus would require large reels to alter the positioning.
Furthermore, it would have a long cord or tether, which would be used to connect the two together. On this cord, there will be an electromagnetically propelled vehicle. Due to the sheer size of the structure, it would also be required to travel at thousands of kilometres per hour This must be big enough to transport the cargo and the passengers together to the station.

ADVANTAGES AND DISADVANTAGES

ADVANTAGES

The production of a space elevator is one that has multiple advantageous effects. For starters the lowered cost of launching into orbit. Although expensive, once it is up and fully running placing costs per kilo of payload to be 2.5% that of a rocket launch. It also increases safety and reliability in comparison to space launches, the absence of high volumes of fuel means reduced danger while launching, and terrestrial weather events would not be a hinderance either, more launches could take place whether it be for humans or delicate equipment like satellites further reducing costs. And even launches beyond geosynchronous orbit have higher cost savings, which means more could go into research and exploration, leading to industries that would not be possible without it. There are also environmental impacts, rockets pollute our atmosphere with fuel, using the space elevator, especially one that runs on renewable energy combats this.

DISADVANTAGES

However, the disadvantages also have significance. One being that it is both incredibly costly, not just for its building for the tether which is proving to be its hardest task but also the costs for things surrounding it like the safety of individuals who use it, as well as control systems necessary to detect the elevators stability and any hazards it may or may not face. As well as considering the cost of maintenance and construction to see whether it is something countries and private companies may find it worth in putting money into. This could potentially take away money that is vital in terrestrial infrastructure. Furthermore, there are issues to do with its construction on the ecosystem, potential effects on wildlife and air quality as well as other things must be conducted regardless of its location on land or sea e.g. the effects on migrating birds for one.

Station . 1,000 km

CARBON NANOTUBES

Carbon nanotubes play a special role in the space elevators design because it's one of the few materials that is well suited for the cable structure. Space elevators require a material with low density and high strength. If the cable were also to have a constant cross-sectional area and reach high altitudes for a geostationary orbit it requires strength of about 63 GPA. Carbon nanotubes fit this, with single or multi-walled nanotubes possessing a strength of about 100GPA.

However, even with such a groundbreaking discovery there are still issues with the model. For example, if there was an error in its production and vacancies appeared even in a single nanotube it would prove dire for its production. It would reduce the tensile strength at least 70% below its actual strength, and if a geosynchronous orbit is to be reached a strength of 63 Gpa is required. Furthermore, just because it has a high maximum strength, does not mean it is the strength that will be obtained. An example is how we have not yet achieved regular glass to possess the same strength as a glass whisker. Same material and yet one has a much higher strength than the other. It is speculated that we would have the same issue with carbon nanotubes.

Station . 100 km

WHY HAS IT NOT BEEN ACHIEVED YET?

Despite recent discoveries the space elevator is still just a concept. The main reason there are several factors to consider in its development. Due to the certain imperfections that would come with carbon nanotubing - which would only increase in severity due to its length and radiation exposure – it means that the actual tether would be from carbon nanotubes being arranged in segments attached too one or more other materials. This would add to its weight and affect it 'taper ratio', a ratio that is determined by the balance between the gravitational force at the base of the elevator and the centrifugal force at the top of it. This ensures the tether is taut and is governed by the tether material. Meaning scientists not only have yet to find a suitable material they also must ensure the taper ratio is achieved at a level that is buildable.

Another major issue is the politics that comes with making a space elevator. A space elevator would be an easy target in wars or terrorist attacks. Building one today would require both military defence and international co-operation both requiring their own heavy costs especially since a single break in the tether would cause heavy damages to the elevator.
